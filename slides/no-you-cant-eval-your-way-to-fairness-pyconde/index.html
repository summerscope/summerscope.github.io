Ask for hands — who's a night owl? Who's an early bird? Count roughly. Then reveal: 'Interesting. Research across European populations consistently finds that people born in spring and summer are more likely to be night owls, while people born in autumn and winter skew toward early birds. The mechanism seems to be photoperiod — the amount of daylight your brain calibrated to in your first weeks of life. [Natale & Adan, Neuroscience Letters 1999; Tonetti et al. 2011, replicated across ~5,700 Italian and Spanish students.] Based on the data in this room: if there are more night owls here, I've just recommended that next year's conference starts and ends an hour later. If more early birds — an hour earlier.' Pause. 'Is that fair?' Then, quietly: 'Also — worth noting that night owls are probably underrepresented in this room right now. It's a morning session. So we're already making a decision based on a skewed sample. But I digress.'

'An app update resets your display settings to default. Bright white background, full brightness. For one person: mild irritation. Thirty seconds to fix it, move on. For someone who gets migraines — around 15% of the population — that same screen, at the wrong moment, is the thing that tips them over. The system made the same decision for both of them. One person spent thirty seconds annoyed. The other spent the afternoon in a dark room. Same action. Same output. Not the same harm. Not even close.'
the problem with proxies

same box. different reality.

  • Black woman ✓
  • American ✓
  • candidate for intervention ✓
  • white house vs group house
Laura Summers PyCon DE & PyData 2026
'Now imagine a system trying to decide who to give extra help to. It looks at demographic data. It sees two young women. Both Black. Both American. By almost every metric the system has access to, they look the same. The algorithm flags both as candidates for positive intervention. One of them grew up in public housing. The other grew up in the White House. This system does not know or have a proxy for socio economic background, or for family stability. And even if it did — what field captures the full texture of a life? What data point tells you whether an intervention will feel like support or like condescension?' Pause.

'The gap between what the system can see and what the person actually experiences — that gap is where most of the harm lives. This does not mean we should not try to measure harm. We absolutely should, and we will. But measurement will always be incomplete. And the incompleteness lands hardest on the people who are already carrying the most.' Move to next section.
