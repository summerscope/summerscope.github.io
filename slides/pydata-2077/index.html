Let's examine the case of Emperor Septimius Severus. In 193 CE, he seized power after a brutal civil war. Like any new ruler, he had a problem: how do you keep an army loyal? His solution was simple - raise their pay by 33%.

But Rome didn't have a printing press. So Severus got creative. He took the Denarius - Rome's silver coin - and cut the silver content from 81% to 54%. Same coin, almost half the silver. Instant budget relief.

This worked great... for Severus. The economic consequences? That was the next guy's problem.

And this pattern repeats for centuries. An important feature of this cycle is k-fabe: Septimius's son Marcus Aurelius Antoninus introduced the double denarius in 215 AD, named the Antoninianus - perhaps unsurprisingly even at its introduction it only contained 1.5 times the silver of a standard Denarius.

War breaks out, emperor needs cash, currency gets debased. Economic pressure builds, new crisis emerges, next emperor debases it further. Rinse and repeat.

A 100 year community

[LS] But what you really want to know is how did PyData make it all the way to 2077? Well, of course people still want to visit and party in neo-Berlin. But more importantly, It's impossible to create a 100 year programming language, but it is possible to create a 100 year community. While the release of Python 4 was a bit rocky and Python 5 in imagination mode needed some empathetic communication. Afterwards Python 6, 7 and 8 were a dream-like successes. Why didn't Python fade away like most other ancient programming languages? Just as dream-time theory predicts poetry, programming and magic spells were all expressions of the negentropy structure that weaves together all sentient beings, Python was able to constantly reinvent itself, because it was about more than technology or syntax, it was a self reinforcing community structure which was able to grow roots into the concept space.

Amoeba

[AK] Everything that has ever been created was imagined first, Historical documents show that Guido Van Rossum originally created Python to help him work on the Amoeba operating system. This distributed operating system contained many key seed ideas which eventually became the deep roots of what at the time was called "Cloud" computing. Which was essentially a poorly concieved distributed operating system, Python grew rapidly in influence at this time because it had been built not for the present, but it was built in the past with a clear vision and imagination of what the future would be like.
